# Dzongkha Language Scope

This library teaches **Bhutanese Dzongkha** (རྫོང་ཁ), the national language of Bhutan. It does **not** teach Standard/Lhasa Tibetan or Chöke (ཆོས་སྐད), the classical liturgical language.

Dzongkha and Tibetan share the **Uchen** (དབུ་ཅན) writing system, but they are distinct spoken languages with different vocabulary, pronunciation, and grammar. Using Tibetan dialect forms in Dzongkha lessons is incorrect.

## Dzongkha vs Standard Tibetan — key contrasts

| Meaning | Use (Dzongkha) | Avoid (Standard Tibetan) |
|---------|----------------|--------------------------|
| Hello | ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ་ལ (kuzuzangpo la) | བཀྲ་ཤིས་བདེ་ལེགས (tashi delek) |
| Thank you | བཀའ་དྲིན་ཆེན་ལ (kadinchen la) | ཐུགས་རྗེ་ཆེ (thukje che) |
| Teacher | དགེ་རྒན (gegen) | སློབ་དཔོན (slobpon) |
| He/she is … | ཁོང … ཨིང / རེད (khong … ing / red) | ཁོང … ཡིན (khong … yin) |

## Copula person mapping (Dzongkha)

| Person | Copula | Example |
|--------|--------|---------|
| First (ང, nga — I) | ཡིན (yin) | ང་དགེ་རྒན་ཡིན། (nga gegen yin — I am a teacher.) |
| Second (ཁྱེད་རང, khyed rang — you) | ཡིན་པ (yinpe) | ཁྱེད་རང་དགེ་རྒན་ཡིན་པ། (khyed rang gegen yinpe — You are a teacher.) |
| Third (ཁོང, khong — he/she) | ཨིང (ing) or རེད (red) | ཁོང་སློབ་གྲ་ཨིང (khong lobda ing — He/She is a student.) |

**Note:** Third-person statements with adjectives often use རེད (red), e.g. ང་གི་ཁྱིམ་ཆེན་པོ་རེད། (nga gi khyim chenpo re — My house is big.) Do **not** use ཡིན (yin) with ཁོང (khong) for "he/she is …" — that pattern belongs to Standard Tibetan, not Dzongkha.

## Romanization

Romanization in this library follows common learner conventions (e.g. kuzuzangpo, gegen, kadinchen). Official Roman Dzongkha may differ slightly; always prioritize Dzongkha script forms from these excerpts.
