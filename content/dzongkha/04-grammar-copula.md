# Dzongkha Copula — yin (to be)

> **Language scope:** Bhutanese Dzongkha (རྫོང་ཁ) only — not Standard/Lhasa Tibetan. See `00-language-scope.md`.

The copula ཡིན (yin) links a subject to a noun or adjective ("X is Y").

## yin — first person copula
- **Script:** ཡིན
- **Romanization:** yin
- **Part of speech:** copula verb
- **Usage:** Used with first person (ང, nga — I): "I am …"
- **Pattern:** ང + [noun/adjective] + ཡིན།
- **Example:** ང་དགེ་རྒན་ཡིན། (nga gegen yin — I am a teacher.)

## yinpe — second person copula
- **Script:** ཡིན་པ
- **Romanization:** yinpe
- **Part of speech:** copula verb
- **Usage:** Used with second person (ཁྱེད་རང, khyed rang — you): "You are …"
- **Pattern:** ཁྱེད་རང + [noun/adjective] + ཡིན་པ།
- **Example:** ཁྱེད་རང་དགེ་རྒན་ཡིན་པ། (khyed rang gegen yinpe — You are a teacher.)

## ing — third person copula
- **Script:** ཨིང
- **Romanization:** ing
- **Part of speech:** copula verb
- **Usage:** Used with third person (ཁོང, khong — he/she): "He/She is …"
- **Pattern:** ཁོང + [noun/adjective] + ཨིང
- **Example:** ཁོང་སློབ་གྲ་ཨིང (khong lobda ing — He/She is a student.)

## red — third person / adjective copula (alternate)
- **Script:** རེད
- **Romanization:** red (re)
- **Part of speech:** copula verb
- **Usage:** Common with adjectives and some third-person statements, e.g. ང་གི་ཁྱིམ་ཆེན་པོ་རེད། (nga gi khyim chenpo re — My house is big.) See also S-O-V lesson.
- **Note:** Do not use ཡིན (yin) with ཁོང (khong) for "he/she is …" — that is Standard Tibetan, not Dzongkha.

## I am a teacher (full sentence)
- **Script:** ང་དགེ་རྒན་ཡིན།
- **Romanization:** nga gegen yin
- **Literal:** I teacher am
- **Natural English:** I am a teacher.
- **Breakdown:**
  - ང (nga) — I
  - དགེ་རྒན (gegen) — teacher
  - ཡིན། (yin) — am (copula)

## I am a student
- **Script:** ང་སློབ་གྲ་ཡིན།
- **Romanization:** nga lobda yin
- **Literal:** I student am
- **Natural English:** I am a student.
