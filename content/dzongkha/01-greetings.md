# Dzongkha Greetings

> **Language scope:** Bhutanese Dzongkha (རྫོང་ཁ) only — not Standard/Lhasa Tibetan. See `00-language-scope.md`.

Common greetings for everyday use. Romanization follows common learner conventions.

## kuzuzangpo (general greeting)
- **Script:** ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ
- **Romanization:** kuzuzangpo
- **Part of speech:** greeting
- **Usage:** Polite general greeting; add ལ (la) for extra respect: ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ་ལ (kuzuzangpo la).
- **Example:** ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ་ལ། (kuzuzangpo la — Hello / Good day.)

## kuzuzangpo la (polite greeting)
- **Script:** ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ་ལ
- **Romanization:** kuzuzangpo la
- **Part of speech:** greeting
- **Usage:** Common polite opening when meeting someone.
- **Example:** ཀུ་ཟུ་བཟང་པོ་ལ། ཁྱེད་རང་ག་འདྲུག (kuzuzangpo la, khyed rang ga dru — Hello, how are you?)

## kadinchen (thank you)
- **Script:** བཀའ་དྲིན་ཆེན
- **Romanization:** kadinchen
- **Part of speech:** expression
- **Usage:** Express gratitude; often with ལ (la): བཀའ་དྲིན་ཆེན་ལ (kadinchen la).
- **Example:** བཀའ་དྲིན་ཆེན་ལ། (kadinchen la — Thank you very much.)

## lekso jay (goodbye)
- **Script:** ལེགས་སོ་རྗེས
- **Romanization:** lekso jay
- **Part of speech:** farewell
- **Usage:** Common parting phrase.
- **Example:** ལེགས་སོ་རྗེས། (lekso jay — Goodbye / See you later.)

## ga dru (how are you)
- **Script:** ག་འདྲུག
- **Romanization:** ga dru
- **Part of speech:** phrase
- **Usage:** Informal "how are you?" — often preceded by ཁྱེད་རང (khyed rang, you).
- **Example:** ཁྱེད་རང་ག་འདྲུག (khyed rang ga dru — How are you?)

## legso re (I am fine)
- **Script:** ལེགས་སོ་རེ
- **Romanization:** legso re
- **Part of speech:** phrase
- **Usage:** Common reply meaning "I am fine" or "all is well."
- **Example:** ལེགས་སོ་རེ། (legso re — I'm fine.)
