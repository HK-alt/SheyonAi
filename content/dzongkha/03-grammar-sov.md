# Dzongkha Sentence Structure — S-O-V

> **Language scope:** Bhutanese Dzongkha (རྫོང་ཁ) only — not Standard/Lhasa Tibetan. See `00-language-scope.md`.

Dzongkha typically follows Subject–Object–Verb (S-O-V) word order.

## Basic S-O-V pattern
- **Rule:** Subject comes first, then Object, then Verb at the end.
- **Pattern:** [Subject] + [Object] + [Verb]
- **Example:** ང་ཆུ་བཏུངས། (nga chu thung — I water drink = I drink water.)
  - Subject: ང (nga — I)
  - Object: ཆུ (chu — water)
  - Verb: བཏུངས། (thung — drink)

## S-O-V with noun object
- **Rule:** The direct object sits between subject and verb.
- **Example:** ང་ཟ་བཟའ། (nga za zay — I food eat = I eat food.)
  - Subject: ང (nga)
  - Object: ཟ (za — food)
  - Verb: བཟའ། (zay — eat)

## Possessive before noun (within S-O-V)
- **Rule:** Possessor + གི (gi) + possessed noun forms a noun phrase that can be subject or object.
- **Example:** ང་གི་ཁྱིམ (nga gi khyim — my house)
- **In a sentence:** ང་གི་ཁྱིམ་ཆེན་པོ་རེད། (nga gi khyim chenpo re — My house is big.)

## Question word order
- **Rule:** Question words often appear where the answer would go; sentence may still end with verb or copula.
- **Example:** ཁྱེད་རང་ག་འདྲུག (khyed rang ga dru — You how-are = How are you?)

## Practice — reorder to S-O-V
- **Words:** ང (nga), ཆུ (chu), བཏུང (thung)
- **Correct order:** ང་ཆུ་བཏུངས། (nga chu thung — I drink water.)
- **Note:** Verb takes past/stem form བཏུངས། in this example.

## Practice — fill in the blank
- **Prompt:** ___ ཆུ ___ (I drink water)
- **Answer:** ང་ཆུ་བཏུངས། (nga chu thung)

## Comparison with English (S-V-O)
- **English:** I (S) drink (V) water (O)
- **Dzongkha:** ང (S) ཆུ (O) བཏུངས། (V)
- **Tip:** Put the verb last when building Dzongkha sentences.
